8

What actually remains

Put it together for Albanian nationals: after every real, checked correction, where does the honest incarceration ratio actually land?

Right where it started — 26.0x to 57.9x for Albanian nationals — because none of the further factors were precisely quantifiable for this nationality. What changed is what you now know that number does and doesn't mean.

26.0–57.9x — the honest, still-uncertain answer

Reproducing the raw calculation from official data does not make the headline number smaller — if anything, the best available (though five-year-stale) population denominator gives a HIGHER ratio than the widely quoted one for Albanian and Afghan nationals. What the raw number can't do is stand as a precise, settled fact: it swings by a factor of two depending on which equally “official” population estimate you plug in, it compares an entire national population against a heavily age/sex-skewed migrant population, and a meaningful share of the foreign-national prison count is unconvicted defendants and people who will be removed from the country long before any long-run reoffending count could capture them. The Migration Observatory's analysis of ALL non-citizens together — the only published like-for-like adjustment that exists — finds that once age and sex are controlled for, non-citizens are actually under-represented in the prison population overall, though this varies by offence type. Whether Albanian, Iranian or Afghan nationals specifically would show the same pattern, a smaller elevation, or something else again is a genuine, currently unanswerable question: no UK statistical body publishes an age/sex-adjusted incarceration rate for individual nationalities. That gap in the public record — not a hidden “true” number we're not telling you — is the honest bottom line. One more honesty note: the ORDER these corrections were applied in is one reasonable choice, not the only one — applying them in a different sequence changes the intermediate numbers shown at each step, though not the final honest range, since every step that couldn't be precisely quantified left the range unchanged regardless of where it sat in the sequence.

Is it true that Albanian nationals in the UK are "16.6 times more likely" to be in prison than British people?

Reproducing the "16.6x more likely to be in prison" calculation from official data — the same kind of prison count divided by the same kind of population estimate — does not make the number smaller. For Albanian and Afghan nationals it comes out larger. The real problem is what a raw ratio like this leaves out. The population figure on the bottom of the sum is a UK statistical series that was discontinued in 2021, with a margin of error of roughly a third even then. "British" in the comparison means the UK's entire population, newborns to pensioners, while migrant nationality groups are overwhelmingly young working-age adults — the highest-imprisonment group for every nationality, not just some. And foreign nationals in the prison count are twice as likely as British nationals to be unconvicted and awaiting trial rather than sentenced, while thousands of convicted foreign offenders are removed from the country each year, before a longer-run count would ever catch them. None of that proves the true, fully corrected gap is zero — for these specific nationalities, no official body has published the like-for-like adjustment needed to know either way. How do conviction rates and prison populations differ between British and foreign nationals?

Migration Observatory, University of Oxford · published 2025-09-30 · retrieved 2026-08-18

The same academic analysis cited by this site's companion briefing (as S5b there), reused here because it is the ONLY published age/sex-adjusted comparison of foreign-national and British incarceration that exists. It analyses non-citizens as ONE pooled group against citizens; it does not, and does not claim to, break the adjustment down by individual nationality.

Quoted directly: “If we control for age and sex, non-citizens are underrepresented in the prison population” (against a raw, unadjusted picture where citizen and non-citizen shares looked roughly similar). This is the honest ceiling of what's publicly available: the best evidence says the OVERALL gap shrinks or reverses after adjustment; whether these three specific nationalities follow that pattern is not publicly known.

Legal status of begging and rough sleeping enforcement

UK Parliament (legislation.gov.uk) and Home Office · published 2026-06-29 · retrieved 2026-08-18

Checked against a further hypothesis: that homelessness/begging enforcement disproportionately affects migrants, and might distort the statistics this page discusses. Two separate legal facts, both primary-sourced: (1) the Vagrancy Act 1824, England and Wales's begging/vagrancy offence since Georgian times, was FULLY REPEALED on 29 June 2026 (Police, Crime, Sentencing and Courts Act 2022, s.81, commenced by S.I. 2026/690) — as of this page's retrieval date it carries no criminal offence at all; modern enforcement instead uses anti-social-behaviour powers (Community Protection Notices, Public Spaces Protection Orders), which the sector expects a further Crime and Policing Bill to reshape. (2) Separately and still in force: since 1 December 2020, rough sleeping has been a discretionary ground under the Immigration Rules for refusing or cancelling a non-British national's permission to stay in the UK (currently Part Suitability, paragraphs SUI 27.1–27.2, formerly Part 9 paragraph 9.21) — amended in April 2021 to require persistent refusal of support plus anti-social behaviour, and explicitly not applicable to those with settled/protection status. This is an enforcement exposure that, by definition, cannot exist for a British national.

Vagrancy Act 1824 repeal, England and Wales: In force from 29 June 2026

Confirms begging/vagrancy has NOT been a criminal offence at all since this date — consistent with S1's finding that no detailed offence code resembling vagrancy or begging appears in the current prisoner tables.

legislation.gov.uk, Police, Crime, Sentencing and Courts Act 2022, s.81, Commencement Information

Sources:https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/32/part/3/crossheading/repeal-of-the-vagrancy-act-1824-etc

Rough sleeping as grounds for immigration refusal/cancellation: In force since 1 December 2020, non-British nationals only

Applies only where a person has repeatedly refused suitable offers of support and engaged in persistent anti-social behaviour — not to rough sleeping alone. Does not apply to those with settled status, indefinite leave, or a protection route (e.g. refugees).

The real effect is on immigration status — a mechanism, not a prison-count distortion — and on the broader statistics in S7 (who gets recorded as homeless), not on the incarceration figures this page's ratios are built from.

What this data can and cannot tell you

  • The population denominator problem is the central limitation of this whole page, not a footnote to it: the UK's only individual-nationality population series was discontinued after 2021, its best available (2024, ad hoc) successor only publishes broad regional groups, and the planned replacement methodology isn't due until November–December 2026. Every ratio computed anywhere — on this page, by a journalist, or by an AI chatbot — for a 2026 headcount against an individual nationality's population currently rests on either a five-year-stale official estimate or an unofficial one nobody has published a methodology for.
  • MoJ's prison tables do not cross-tabulate age against nationality at all — not even at the British/foreign group level, let alone for individual nationalities — so this page cannot present a direct, computed age-standardised incarceration rate for Albanian, Iranian or Afghan nationals. Step 3's small-boat age/sex data is offered as the best-evidenced, most current available context for the underlying mechanism, not as a substitute for that missing statistic.
  • MoJ prison figures are England and Wales only; the ONS population denominator (S2) is UK-wide. Wales's Albanian and Iranian nationality counts are small enough to be marked “No contacts” (Albania) or partially suppressed in the source table, so an England-and-Wales-only denominator could not be reliably constructed without guessing at suppressed cells; using the UK-wide figure instead means the true England-and-Wales-specific population is probably slightly SMALLER than shown here, which would make the true ratio slightly HIGHER — a conservative direction, not one that favours this page's argument.
  • Four further hypotheses about what might inflate or deflate these figures were checked directly against primary data and are reported honestly whichever way they came out: sex-work enforcement (S1) and pure immigration-status offences (S1) both turned out to be NEGLIGIBLE contributors to the current sentenced-prisoner count; the remand disparity (S1) and deportation attrition (S4) both turned out to be REAL and are built into the interactive walkthrough's steps 4 and 6; homelessness/begging enforcement (S6, S7) turned out to be a real, nationality-specific mechanism but one that affects immigration status and homelessness statistics, not the prison-population figures this page's ratios are built from.
  • The interactive walkthrough applies its corrections in one particular order (denominator uncertainty, then age/sex, then remand, then immigration-status offences, then deportation, then enforcement intensity). This is one reasonable ordering, not the only one — a different order would show different numbers at each intermediate step, though the final honest range is unaffected, since every step that could not be precisely quantified left the range unchanged regardless of where it sat in the sequence.
  • This page checks whether raw nationality-incarceration ratios are misleading; it does not and cannot determine why any genuine, fully-adjusted gap that might remain would exist — offending behaviour, policing practice, offence mix, poverty, displacement and asylum-system pressures are all live, contested, and not resolved by anything on this page.
  • As with this site's companion briefing on UK crime by nationality: every nationality figure here is a legal-nationality count, not an immigration-status or ethnicity count. Every table's small “not recorded” category is shown on this page, not silently dropped.

Bleep publishes real data from primary sources — every figure traced to an official document, archived, with honest limits stated alongside it. Nothing here is fabricated or cherry-picked.
