What can be checked, and what cannot

The claim that Afghan men in Britain are 22 times more likely to be convicted of rape is argued almost entirely with figures that either cannot be traced or cannot be divided by anything reliable. Here is the line between what can be checked and what cannot, with the source for each side of it.

What the data does show

  • 43 sexual-offence convictions of Afghan nationals in England and Wales in 2024 — 19 for sexual assault on a female, 8 for rape of a female aged 16 or over. Counts, not rates.

How do conviction rates and prison populations differ between British and foreign nationals?

Migration Observatory, University of Oxford · published 2025-09-30 · retrieved 2026-08-18

The only published age- and sex-adjusted comparison in this area. It pools all non-citizens as a single group and finds that "if we control for age and sex, non-citizens are underrepresented in the prison population." That is a pooled, group-level result; the Observatory does not extend it to individual nationalities, and it cannot be read as an Afghan finding. It is included because it establishes that the age and sex adjustment is large enough to reverse a raw comparison — and no such adjustment can be made for Afghan nationals specifically, because MoJ does not publish prisoner age by nationality at all.

The Afghan population in the UK is, per the resettlement data on this page, heavily weighted towards recent arrivals, and migration routes skew towards young working-age men — the highest-imprisonment demographic for every nationality including British. That plausibly applies here, but no Afghan-specific adjustment has been computed, because no published data allows one to be computed precisely. Direction known, size unknown.

What this data can and cannot tell you

  • No Afghan per-person rate is given anywhere on this page, deliberately. The UK's own population data answers "how many Afghans are here?" two different and both-legitimate ways — 18,000 by current nationality, 47,000 by country of birth — and those differ by a factor of 2.6. Both are from 2021 and both predate the post-evacuation arrivals. Any rate built on either would carry an unquantifiable and probably large bias, and a precise-looking number would misrepresent how confident anyone can honestly be.
  • The original analysis behind the "22 times" claim was not located, so its exact mechanism is not pinned down. Three secondary reconstructions disagree with each other about how the figure was built; that disagreement is reported here rather than resolved, and none of the three is asserted over the others.
  • The authenticity of the 2024 conviction data is substantially, not fully, confirmed. LBC, a mainstream broadcaster, independently reported identical figures with its own byline one day after the Centre for Migration Control first published them, which is strong corroboration — but the freedom-of-information response document itself has not been obtained from the Ministry of Justice, so some uncertainty about its exact wording and scope remains.
  • Two fact-checks of the "22 times" claim could not be read directly. Sky News's page returned "Access Denied" to a real browser, and BBC News articles were found blocked in the same way, so both are characterised from search-index snippets rather than read. BBC Radio 4's "More or Less" did broadcast a dedicated episode asking whether Afghan nationals are more likely to be convicted of sexual offences, on 10 September 2025 — the programme page loaded and confirms it — but the audio is marked not currently available and no transcript is published, so it confirms mainstream attention to the question and nothing more.
  • The 85,696-versus-47,000 discrepancy in Afghan country-of-birth figures is resolved, not outstanding. Both are real: 85,696 is a census full count of England and Wales on one day, 47,000 is a UK-wide survey estimate with a ±12,000 sampling error. The gap between them is further evidence that the survey-vintage figure understates the true population.
  • Where a figure on this page IS elevated, this page does not claim to explain why. Age and sex structure, remand and charging disparities, and the effect of removing foreign national offenders are all documented at the level of foreign nationals as a group, not by individual nationality, because the government does not publish them that way. Where this page notes that a group-level finding plausibly extends to Afghan nationals, that is a direction, not a measurement.
  • The comparison with other nationalities is not exhaustive. Seven were computed — French, German, Italian, Polish, Portuguese, Romanian and Spanish — because their UK populations are measured well enough to divide by, and because they answer the specific "what about ordinary European nationalities" question. Every other nationality in the same ONS and Ministry of Justice tables, including several with sizeable prison headcounts, was not computed. A full nationality-by-nationality table would be a separate piece of work.
  • Ethnicity is not nationality, and the two are routinely conflated in claims about this subject. The Ministry of Justice records ethnicity of offenders as an entirely separate variable from nationality: someone can be recorded as Asian by ethnicity and British by nationality, or the reverse. ONS's own responses confirm it holds no crime data broken down by asylum or migrant status at all.
  • Convictions are not offences. A conviction count reflects what was reported, investigated, charged, prosecuted and proved — every stage of which has its own attrition, and none of which is published broken down by nationality.

Bleep publishes real data from primary sources — every figure traced to an official document, archived, with honest limits stated alongside it. Nothing here is fabricated or cherry-picked.
