The Hamas Covenant vs Israeli Founding & Governing Manifestos

Forensically comparing declared doctrines, constitutional declarations, and party platforms across 5 core dimensions: territorial sovereignty, religious vs democratic state identity, peace and negotiations, treatment of minorities, and modern doctrinal evolution.

Dimension 1 of 5

Territorial Sovereignty ('River to Sea')

Both founding party platforms assert exclusive sovereignty over 100% of the land between the Jordan River and Mediterranean Sea, rejecting the other's right to an independent state.

Hamas Stated Position 1988 Covenant & 2017 Document

Indivisible Islamic Waqf from Jordan River to Mediterranean Sea; no concessions permitted (1988 Art. 11). Accepted 1967 borders as national consensus in 2017 while maintaining historic claim (2017 Art. 20).

Israeli Founding & Governing Stance 1948 Declaration, Likud Platforms & 2022 Guidelines

'Between the Sea and the Jordan there will only be Israeli sovereignty' (Likud 1977). 'The Jewish people have an exclusive and inalienable right to all parts of the Land of Israel' (37th Govt Guidelines 2022).

Dimension 2 of 5

Ideological & Religious Character

Hamas (1988) is an apocalyptic Islamist theocratic charter quoting the Protocols of Zion; Israel's 1948 Declaration is a secular-democratic charter pledging civil equality; modern Israeli laws assert exclusive Jewish national rights.

Hamas Stated Position 1988 Covenant & 2017 Document

Theocratic Islamic state governed by Sharia. Non-Muslims tolerated under 'shadow of Islam' (1988). Antisemitic slurs removed and decoupled from Judaism in 2017 (Arts. 16-17).

1948 Declaration pledges 'complete equality of social and political rights irrespective of religion, race or sex'. 2018 Basic Law declares self-determination 'unique to the Jewish people'.

Dimension 3 of 5

Peace, Negotiations & Partition

Hamas (1988) rejected all diplomacy in favor of armed Jihad; Likud rejects a Palestinian state west of Jordan while signing regional peace accords; 1948 Declaration accepted UN partition.

Hamas Stated Position 1988 Covenant & 2017 Document

Rejects international conferences and peace plans as apostasy; Jihad is only solution (1988 Art. 13). Reaffirmed armed struggle as strategic choice in 2017 (Art. 23).

Israeli Founding & Governing Stance 1948 Declaration, Likud Platforms & 2022 Guidelines

1948 Declaration accepted UN Partition 181 and extended hand of peace. Likud (1977/1999) flatly rejects Palestinian statehood, but signed peace with Egypt (1979) and Gulf states (2020).

Dimension 4 of 5

Treatment of Minorities & The Other

Hamas (1988) called for violent elimination of the Zionist entity and dhimmi status; modern Israeli governance maintains military control over stateless Palestinians while Arab citizens have voting rights in Israel proper.

Hamas Stated Position 1988 Covenant & 2017 Document

1988 Covenant contained overt antisemitic hadiths (Art. 7) and conspiracism. 2017 Document rejected anti-Jewish religious hatred, focusing strictly on Zionist occupation.

Israeli Founding & Governing Stance 1948 Declaration, Likud Platforms & 2022 Guidelines

1948 Declaration offered full citizenship to Arab inhabitants. In practice, millions of Palestinians in West Bank/Gaza live under military rule without citizenship or voting rights.

Pragmatic Trajectory & Modern Evolution

Hamas Stated Position 1988 Covenant & 2017 Document

Israeli Founding & Governing Stance 1948 Declaration, Likud Platforms & 2022 Guidelines

How does the Hamas Covenant compare to Israeli founding and governing manifestos?

When comparing the 1988 Hamas Covenant to Israeli founding and governing documents, the textual evidence reveals a striking territorial symmetry alongside stark ideological divergences. On territorial claims, both dominant governing movements assert exclusive sovereignty over the entire land "from the river to the sea": Hamas's 1988 Covenant declares all of Palestine an indivisible Islamic Waqf where any territorial compromise is religious apostasy, while the Likud Party's founding 1977 platform explicitly declared that "between the Sea and the Jordan there will only be Israeli sovereignty"—a doctrine reaffirmed by the 37th Government's 2022 guidelines asserting an "exclusive and inalienable right" over the entire Land of Israel. The sharpest contrast lies in founding principles: Israel's 1948 Declaration of Independence is a secular-democratic charter that explicitly pledges complete equality of social and political rights to all inhabitants irrespective of religion or race and accepted UN partition, whereas Hamas's 1988 Covenant is an apocalyptic theocratic text invoking the Protocols of the Elders of Zion, calling for armed Jihad to obliterate Israel, and quoting hadiths on killing Jews. Both movements have since evolved: Hamas's 2017 Document formally disavowed religious antisemitism and accepted the 1967 borders as a formula of national consensus (while refusing to recognize Israel), whereas Israeli governing law shifted rightward with the 2018 Basic Law declaring self-determination "unique to the Jewish people" and the 2022 coalition expanding settlement annexation. Did Likud use the phrase 'between the river and the sea' before Hamas?

Yes. In its original 1977 electoral platform, the Likud coalition (led by Menachem Begin) explicitly stated: 'between the Sea and the Jordan there will only be Israeli sovereignty'—published eleven years before Hamas was founded during the First Intifada in 1988 and issued its Covenant declaring all of Palestine from the Jordan River to the Mediterranean an Islamic Waqf. While the phrase 'from the river to the sea' is today predominantly associated with Palestinian nationalism and pro-Palestinian slogans, territorial maximalism claiming the entire land between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea has been foundational doctrine for the dominant governing party of Israel since 1977.

While both platforms claim the entirety of the territory, Likud proposed limited personal autonomy for Arab residents under Israeli security control, whereas Hamas's 1988 Covenant called for the violent destruction of Israel and the subjugation of non-Muslims under Sharia rule.

What this data can and cannot tell you

  • Declared doctrine vs operational reality: Official charters outline ideological commitments and policy stances; they do not fully describe battlefield actions, clandestine diplomacy, or pragmatic accommodations that occur in practice.
  • Legal weight varies across documents: Israel's Declaration of Independence is an interpretive constitutional charter; Basic Laws hold quasi-constitutional supremacy; Likud platforms and the Hamas Covenant are political party/movement manifestos; and coalition guidelines are executive contracts.
  • Neither Israeli nor Palestinian political society is a monolith: The texts compared here represent the dominant nationalist/governing wings (Hamas on one side, Likud and the religious-nationalist coalition on the other), but do not encompass liberal-Zionist parties advocating a two-state solution or Palestinian factions (such as Fatah/PLO) that formally recognized Israel in 1993.
  • Status of revisions: While Hamas issued its 2017 Document of General Principles updating its political doctrine, it never formally repealed or revoked its 1988 Covenant; similarly, while Likud has never officially repudiated its 1977 platform, Prime Minister Netanyahu has at various times verbally acknowledged conditional demilitarized Palestinian statehood (e.g. 2009 Bar-Ilan speech) before subsequently disavowing it.
  • Translations and terminology: Texts originating in Arabic and Hebrew are quoted from standard, widely accepted English legal and academic translations (Yale Avalon Project, Knesset official translation, Jewish Virtual Library, Adalah).
