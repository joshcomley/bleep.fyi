What do the official statistics actually say about Albanian nationals and crime in the UK?

The most-quoted claim about Albanian nationals in Britain — that they are the nationality most likely to commit sexual offences — cannot be checked against official UK data, because the government has never published the figure it would take. The Ministry of Justice publishes how many Albanian nationals are in prison (943, as at 30 June 2026) and separately how many prisoners are held for sexual offences, but it has never crossed the two, for Albanians or for any other nationality. The one dataset that would answer it has been compiled by the Ministry, ordered released by the Information Commissioner, and withheld while the Ministry appeals. Where individual police forces have published their own full nationality tables, Albanians are near the bottom: 2 of the 575 arrests of non-UK nationals for rape and sexual assault in West Yorkshire in 2024, and 2 of the 594 West Midlands sexual-offence records that name a nationality in 2025. What is true is that Albanian nationals are the largest single foreign nationality in prison in England and Wales, and on the raw overall imprisonment rate — all offences, not sexual offences — they are the highest of any nationality that can be measured, at 35.9 times the British rate. That figure carries two separate kinds of uncertainty, and both are large. Its own survey's margin of error alone puts it between 26.0 and 57.9 times. Swapping that survey for a different official population figure — there are six, all defensible — moves it further still, to anywhere between 8.3 and 57.9 times. During 2025, 1,937 Albanian nationals were referred to the government as suspected victims of modern slavery — about twice the 943 in prison — and just over half of those referrals involved criminal exploitation: being trafficked into committing crimes for someone else. What the official record does and does not contain

Almost every argument about Albanian nationals and crime turns on figures the government has never published. This page separates what can actually be checked from what cannot, and names the source behind every item.

What the data does show

  • 943 Albanian nationals were in prison in England and Wales on 30 June 2026 — the largest single foreign nationality, ahead of Irish 684, Romanian 621 and Polish 621.

    MoJ, Offender management statistics quarterly, Table 1.Q.12

    Sources:https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/offender-management-statistics-quarterly-january-to-march-2026

  • The widely-quoted 35.9-times figure — Albanian prisoners per head against British prisoners per head — is built on a mismatch: today's Albanian prisoner count divided by a population estimate frozen at 2021, the year that prisoner count peaked at 1,528. It has fallen 38% since, to 943, while Albanian migration to the UK rose sharply over the same period.

    MoJ, Offender management statistics quarterly, Table 1.A.28

    Sources:https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/offender-management-statistics-quarterly-january-to-march-2026

  • Among people convicted and serving a sentence for a sexual offence, foreign nationals are slightly under-represented (8.66%, against a 9.19% share of all sentenced prisoners).

    MoJ, Offender management statistics quarterly, Table 1.A.27

    Sources:https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/offender-management-statistics-quarterly-january-to-march-2026

  • In West Yorkshire in 2024, 2 of the 575 arrests of non-UK nationals for rape and sexual assault were of Albanian nationals. In the West Midlands in 2025, 2 of the 594 sexual-offence offender records that name a nationality were Albanian.

    West Yorkshire Police FOI 2355119-25; West Midlands Police FOI 1076/26

    Sources:https://www.westyorkshire.police.uk/freedom-of-information/february-2025-foi-2355119-25-sexual-crimes-committed-non-uk-nationals

What the data cannot show

  • How many Albanian nationals are in prison for a sexual offence. The Ministry of Justice's only table carrying offence type breaks nationality into four values — All, British, Foreign, Not recorded — so no figure for any individual nationality exists.

    MoJ, Offender management statistics quarterly, Table 1.A.27 and Contents sheet

    Sources:https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/offender-management-statistics-quarterly-january-to-march-2026

  • How many Albanian nationals are convicted each year, of anything. The Ministry of Justice's routine conviction statistics carry no nationality field at all, and the fuller dataset that would answer it was ordered released by the Information Commissioner in June 2026 and is still being withheld while the Ministry appeals that order to a tribunal.

What this data can and cannot tell you

  • There is no published figure anywhere for how many Albanian nationals are convicted of, or imprisoned for, a sexual offence — so the question people argue about cannot be answered from published data, and this page does not construct an answer. The closest thing that exists is the Metropolitan Police's count of people charged with sexual offences in London by nationality: it is heavily suppressed, it counts charges rather than convictions, and its rate becomes uninterpretable once the population estimate's own uncertainty is propagated.
  • Every estimate of how many Albanian nationals live in the UK is wrong in a known direction and an unknown size, and the direction is UNDER-count: each available figure is likely to be lower than the true number, which makes every ratio built on it higher than the truth. The survey estimate of 21,000 is a discontinued series with a 38% margin of error; the 2021 census counted 68,673 Albanian-born residents of England and Wales, which is a hard count of a different thing — country of birth, not nationality — and is five years stale, predating the 2022 surge in Albanian arrivals entirely. The spread between them is a factor of three, and every ratio on this page divides by one of them.
  • Prison figures are a snapshot, not a flow. A one-day headcount understates how many people pass through the system in a year — particularly for Albanian nationals, 1,557 of whom were returned from the UK as foreign national offenders in the year to March 2026, more than the 943 Albanian nationals held in prison at the most recent snapshot (30 June 2026).
  • About a third of foreign nationals in prison have not been convicted of anything. 32.40% are on remand awaiting trial, against 15.77% of British nationals — and for sexual offences the gap is wider still: 31.01% of foreign nationals held for a sexual offence are on remand against a 21.34% share of all remand prisoners. Prisoner counts used as a proxy for offending therefore over-count foreign nationals, but MoJ publishes no remand rate by individual nationality, so the size of that correction for Albanians cannot be computed.
  • No ratio on this page is adjusted for age or sex. The British comparator is the entire British population, newborns to pensioners, while Albanian nationals in the UK are overwhelmingly young adult men — the highest-imprisonment demographic for every nationality, including British. The Ministry of Justice does not cross age with nationality at any level, so an age-standardised Albanian rate cannot be constructed from published data at all. The only published age- and sex-adjusted comparison pools all non-citizens together and finds they are under-represented once adjusted; that is a pooled finding about non-citizens as a group, not an Albanian one, and this page does not present it as one.
  • Where the Metropolitan Police's table of sexual-offence charges by nationality shows a count below five, it replaces the number with an asterisk — this is what produces the Albanian 26–41 five-year range cited in the featured answer. Each of those hidden cells is treated here as between 1 and 4, which is the correct bound — but it means every suppressed nationality's five-year total is a range rather than a figure, and ranges do not combine into precise ratios.
  • Arrests and charges count events, not people. Repeat arrests of one person inflate the counts; West Yorkshire Police states this explicitly in its own disclosure. No published source deduplicates.
  • Nationality recording is poor. Nearly half of West Midlands Police's sexual-offence offender records carry no nationality at all, and the same force logged "English" as a nationality separate from "British" — an uncontrolled free-text field. Nationality in police and prison systems is largely self-declared.
  • Ethnicity findings are not nationality findings. The best evidence on how differently the justice system treats different groups is recorded by ethnicity, because nationality is not collected — the Ministry of Justice's own conclusion is that "the greatest disparity appears at the point of stop and search, custodial remands and prison population". That is the closest available proxy for a nationality effect, not the thing itself.
  • Three things were looked for and not obtained, listed so nobody assumes they were checked: the Centre for Migration Control's own underlying FOI data and methodology (paywalled, with the raw force-by-force returns not published anywhere located); the Office for Statistics Regulation's internal briefing on media coverage of sexual offences in London by foreign nationals (its existence is confirmed in an ONS FOI response, its content has never been published); and any First-tier Tribunal decision on the Ministry of Justice's appeal against the Information Commissioner's order to release conviction data by nationality and offence (none found as at 18 August 2026).
  • No causal claim is made anywhere on this page. Where it sets out a reason the numbers look the way they do — removals emptying the prison count, remand inflating it, the age and sex profile of recent migrants, trafficking into forced criminality — each is a factor whose DIRECTION is evidenced. In almost every case the SIZE is not published, and this page does not estimate one. An honest "we cannot tell" is the finding.
  • Two of the press claims this page tests are archived only in part. The Telegraph article is behind a paywall, so its headline, sub-headline and opening line are in the archived capture but the rest of its body is not; the arrest counts and the fuller nationality ranking printed there could not be archived, and are not quoted here as though they had been. Where a figure from that analysis matters, it is cited to the GB News report of the same analysis, which is archived in full.

Bleep publishes real data from primary sources — every figure traced to an official document, archived, with honest limits stated alongside it. Nothing here is fabricated or cherry-picked.
