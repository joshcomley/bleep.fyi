Every figure below is traced to an official document, archived on this page, with a retrieval date. Where the official record has a gap or a live dispute, that is stated as plainly as the figures themselves — see what this data can and cannot tell you.

Updated 2026-08-17

For the comment thread

Yes — official UK data broken down by nationality exists. The Ministry of Justice publishes the prison population by nationality every quarter: as at 30 June 2026, foreign nationals were 11.8% of the prison population (10,134 of 85,858), and 10.25% of prisoners held for sexual offences specifically — slightly under-represented on that measure. What the government does NOT routinely publish is criminal convictions broken down by nationality; MoJ has confirmed in writing to Parliament that its conviction statistics carry no nationality breakdown, and MoJ is currently appealing an Information Commissioner's Office order to release fuller conviction-by-nationality data. Full sources, verified figures and archived copies of every spreadsheet: https://bleep.fyi/r/uk-crime-by-nationality

ICO decision notice IC-416928-D9J8 (Ministry of Justice)

Information Commissioner's Office · published 2026-06-22 · retrieved 2026-08-17

This is the honest answer to "why is conviction-by-nationality data hard to find", assembled from three verified primary documents rather than one release. (1) The ICO ruling: MoJ refused an FOI request for total convictions 2018-2024 broken down by nationality and offence group, citing cost and personal-data exemptions; the Commissioner upheld the complaint on both points against MoJ (i.e. ordered fuller disclosure). Per press reporting corroborated against this notice's own reference and date (not stated on the notice itself), the requester was the Centre for Migration Control and MoJ has appealed to the First-tier Tribunal — so the fuller 2018-2024 data remains unpublished. (2) A Home Office written answer to Parliament (18 July 2025, UIN 66370) states plainly that MoJ's routine conviction-outcomes data "is not broken down by nationality or immigration status." (3) An ONS FOI response confirms ONS itself holds no offender-by-nationality data at all, because its crime statistics are victim-based (CSEW) or police-recorded, not offender-based.

A widely-repeated press figure — that foreign nationals accounted for 14.1% of sexual-offence convictions in a recent year, citing MoJ data drawn from the Police National Computer — could not be independently traced to an openable primary government document. It is press-reported only (multiple outlets, all citing the same unpublished MoJ/PNC extraction) and is not presented here as a verified fact. Separately, several different percentages (14.1%, 15%, 22-26%, "up to a quarter") circulate in the press for different periods and definitions, sourced to FOI releases obtained by the Centre for Migration Control rather than one consistent official statistical series — which is itself the point: there is no single official, routinely-published conviction-by-nationality figure to check those numbers against, and the ICO dispute above is exactly the live fight over releasing one.

Criminal Justice Statistics Quarterly: March 2026

Ministry of Justice · published 2026-07-30 · retrieved 2026-08-17

MoJ's main prosecutions/convictions/sentencing statistics tool. It is included here for a negative finding: the "Outcomes by Offence" pivot data tool, covering 12 months ending March 2017 through 12 months ending March 2026, can be filtered by Quarter, Month, Person/Other, Sex, Age Group, Age Range, Ethnicity and Detailed Ethnicity — there is no nationality field anywhere in the tool. This is the routine, regularly-published court-outcomes series, and it simply does not capture nationality. That gap is exactly why the prison tables (S1) and the contested FOI/ICO data (S3) are the sources that exist for this question, rather than the main conviction statistics.

Breakdown dimensions available in MoJ's routine court-outcomes tool: Quarter, Month, Person/Other, Sex, Age Group, Age Range, Ethnicity, Detailed Ethnicity — no nationality

Demographic fields including ethnicity are noted as sparsely recorded for summary offences, since many never require a court appearance.

How do conviction rates and prison populations differ between British and foreign nationals?

Migration Observatory, University of Oxford · published 2025-09-30 · retrieved 2026-08-17

Academic analysis of the same government prison-population data used in S1, by researchers Ben Brindle and Madeleine Sumption. This is University of Oxford analysis, not a government source itself — but it is the clearest published explanation of why a raw nationality share is not the same as an offending rate.

Quoted directly: "If we do not control for age, the share of non-citizens receiving convictions (13%) or in prison (12.4%) in 2024 ... appears to be roughly similar to the share of non-citizens in the age 16+ population ... around 12% in 2024 ... If we control for age and sex, non-citizens are underrepresented in the prison population." Also: "Convictions and incarceration rates vary by offence type ... Non-citizens are overrepresented among convicted or incarcerated offenders for drug offences, for example, while underrepresented among those convicted or incarcerated for robbery or violence against the person." And: prison data records nationality with "a small share of people (around 0.4% in June 2025) whose nationality is not recorded."

What this data can and cannot tell you

  • Police recorded crime and the Crime Survey for England and Wales (CSEW) — the two main measures of crime in Britain, and the source of the falling-crime trend the original Independent article cited — do not record offender nationality at all. CSEW measures victims' experience of crime; police recorded crime measures reported/detected offences. Neither is an offender database.
  • The only routinely-published, nationality-broken-down official series is the MoJ prison population (source S1) — a snapshot of who is currently in custody, not a flow of convictions, and not the same population as "foreign national offenders removed from the UK" (S2) or "people convicted of a crime" (S3).
  • A raw nationality SHARE of the prison population is not the same as an offending RATE. The foreign-national population in the UK skews younger and more male than the general population, and young men of any nationality are more likely to be convicted of most offence types. The Migration Observatory's analysis (S5b) finds that once age and sex are controlled for, non-citizens are under-represented in the prison population, though this varies by offence type.
  • MoJ's main, routinely-published conviction and sentencing statistics (the "Outcomes by Offence" tool, source S4) do not include a nationality field at all — only offence, sex, age and ethnicity. This is a genuine, confirmed gap in the regular statistical series, not an oversight in this page's research.
  • Fuller conviction-by-nationality data (2018-2024) has been requested under Freedom of Information, and the Information Commissioner ordered the Ministry of Justice to disclose it on 22 June 2026 (source S3) — but the MoJ is, as of the retrieval date above, appealing that order, so the fuller dataset remains unpublished. The specific press figure of "14.1% of sexual offence convictions" could not be traced to an open, primary government document, and is not presented here as verified.
  • Prison and offender-return figures record legal NATIONALITY, not immigration status — a foreign national can hold indefinite leave to remain, refugee status, or British citizenship pending, and a British national is by definition excluded from every "foreign national" figure on this page regardless of their background. Nationality is not a proxy for recent migration.
  • Every table on this page carries its own small "nationality not recorded" category (0.27% of the prison population as at 30 June 2026; around 0.4% in a June 2025 snapshot per the Migration Observatory) — a genuine data-quality gap, not a rounding artefact, and it is included in the figures above rather than silently dropped.
