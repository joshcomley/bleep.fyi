Where are the official UK government statistics on crime broken down by nationality?

Official UK government data on crime and nationality does exist, but it is scattered and easy to miss. The Ministry of Justice publishes the prison population by nationality every quarter: as at 30 June 2026, foreign nationals were 11.8% of the prison population (10,134 of 85,858), and 10.25% of prisoners held for sexual offences specifically — slightly under-represented on that measure. What the government does NOT routinely publish is criminal convictions broken down by nationality: MoJ has confirmed in writing to Parliament that its conviction statistics carry no nationality breakdown, and MoJ is currently appealing an Information Commissioner's Office order to release fuller conviction-by-nationality data. Do foreign nationals commit more sex crimes?

No — foreign nationals are slightly UNDER-represented among prisoners held for sexual offences, not over-represented. As at 30 June 2026, foreign nationals were 10.25% of sexual-offence prisoners: a ratio of 0.87× against their own 11.80% overall share of the prison population, and 0.86× against the 11.88% non-UK share of the general population. Both comparisons tell the same story.

This figure is not inflated by how sex-work offences are counted: soliciting and brothel-keeping contribute zero sentenced prisoners in this data, and exploitation-of-prostitution offences are classified outside the "sexual offences" group entirely. What prison shares cannot establish

Prison representation reflects offending, policing, charging, bail and remand decisions, sentencing, and time served. The figures above cannot separate those effects from each other. The research below is the best official evidence on whether the justice system itself treats people differently at the same stage — and it must be read alongside the representation numbers above, not filed underneath them as a footnote.

Police contact

  • The routine Home Office statistics on stop and search and arrests carry no nationality breakdown at all — only ethnicity. There is no official way to check whether foreign nationals are stopped or arrested at a different rate than their population share, because the data simply is not collected that way.

    Home Office, Stop and search, arrests, and mental health detentions, March 2025, published 6 November 2025

    Sources:https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/stop-and-search-arrests-and-mental-health-detentions-march-2025

Charging and sentencing

  • MoJ's own 2024 ethnicity statistics find disparity widening at each successive stage of the justice pipeline, with the largest gaps at stop and search, custodial remand and the prison population itself. MoJ's own caveat: this cannot show whether ethnicity itself, rather than related factors, causes the difference.

    Ministry of Justice, Ethnicity and the Criminal Justice System 2024, published 27 November 2025, Figure 1.01

    Sources:https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/ethnicity-and-the-criminal-justice-system-2024

  • The 2017 Lammy Review, and the Ministry of Justice analysis behind it, found the odds of imprisonment overall for Black, Asian and Minority Ethnic offenders were 2.4 times the odds for White offenders — an ethnicity finding, not a nationality one, using 2015 data.

    Lammy Review: final report, published 8 September 2017, MoJ analysis cited via source S10

    Sources:https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/lammy-review

  • For drug supply offences specifically, Sentencing Council research found Black offenders had 40% higher odds, and Asian and "Other" ethnicity offenders 50% higher odds, of an immediate custodial sentence than White offenders with comparable case factors — at the Crown Court, using 2012-2015 data.

    Sentencing Council, Investigating the association between an offender's sex and ethnicity and the sentence imposed at the Crown Court for drug offences, published 15 January 2020

    Sources:https://sentencingcouncil.org.uk/publications/research-reports/investigating-the-association-between-an-offender-s-sex-and-ethnicity-and-the-sentence-imposed-at-the-crown-court-for-drug-offences/

Custody before conviction

  • Foreign nationals are 21.3% of the REMAND (pre-conviction, presumed-innocent) prison population but only 9.2% of the SENTENCED population — against an overall 11.8% share. Remand is not a finding of guilt: it typically turns on bail-risk factors such as a fixed UK address or flight risk, which a non-British national may more often lack the ties to satisfy a court on.

    Ministry of Justice, Offender management statistics quarterly, Table 1.A.24, as at 30 June 2026

    Sources:https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/offender-management-statistics-quarterly-january-to-march-2026

All of the disparity research above — the Lammy Review, MoJ's 2024 ethnicity statistics, stop-and-search and arrest data, Sentencing Council research — records ETHNICITY, not NATIONALITY. These are different categories: British nationals of Black, Asian, Mixed or Other ethnicity are the overwhelming majority of people in this research. It identifies real mechanisms in how the justice system operates, but it cannot prove that a NATIONALITY difference (the subject of this page) has the same cause as an ETHNICITY difference. The missing nationality breakdown at most stages of the justice system is itself an evidence gap, not a minor footnote.

ICO decision notice IC-416928-D9J8 (Ministry of Justice)

Information Commissioner's Office · published 2026-06-22 · retrieved 2026-08-17

This is the honest answer to "why is conviction-by-nationality data hard to find", assembled from three verified primary documents rather than one release. (1) The ICO ruling: MoJ refused an FOI request for total convictions 2018-2024 broken down by nationality and offence group, citing cost and personal-data exemptions; the Commissioner upheld the complaint on both points against MoJ (i.e. ordered fuller disclosure). Per press reporting corroborated against this notice's own reference and date (not stated on the notice itself), the requester was the Centre for Migration Control and MoJ has appealed to the First-tier Tribunal — so the fuller 2018-2024 data remains unpublished. (2) A Home Office written answer to Parliament (18 July 2025, UIN 66370) states plainly that MoJ's routine conviction-outcomes data "is not broken down by nationality or immigration status." (3) An ONS FOI response confirms ONS itself holds no offender-by-nationality data at all, because its crime statistics are victim-based (CSEW) or police-recorded, not offender-based.

A widely-repeated press figure — that foreign nationals accounted for 14.1% of sexual-offence convictions in a recent year, citing MoJ data drawn from the Police National Computer — could not be independently traced to an openable primary government document. It is press-reported only (multiple outlets, all citing the same unpublished MoJ/PNC extraction) and is not presented here as a verified fact. Separately, several different percentages (14.1%, 15%, 22-26%, "up to a quarter") circulate in the press for different periods and definitions, sourced to FOI releases obtained by the Centre for Migration Control rather than one consistent official statistical series — which is itself the point: there is no single official, routinely-published conviction-by-nationality figure to check those numbers against, and the ICO dispute above is exactly the live fight over releasing one.

How do conviction rates and prison populations differ between British and foreign nationals?

Migration Observatory, University of Oxford · published 2025-09-30 · retrieved 2026-08-17

Academic analysis of the same government prison-population data used in S1, by researchers Ben Brindle and Madeleine Sumption. Lammy Review: final report

Ministry of Justice (independent review chaired by David Lammy MP) · published 2017-09-08 · retrieved 2026-08-17

An independent government-commissioned review (closed, final report published 8 September 2017) of the treatment of, and outcomes for, Black, Asian and Minority Ethnic (BAME) individuals in the Criminal Justice System — again an ETHNICITY review, not a nationality one. Its most-cited statistic, quoted here via the Sentencing Council's own 2020 paper (source S10), which cites the underlying MoJ analysis directly: the odds of imprisonment overall for Black, Asian and Minority Ethnic offenders were found to be 2.4 times the odds for White offenders. This page does not re-derive that figure independently; it is reported here as MoJ/Sentencing Council's own characterisation of the review's headline finding, not as this page's own analysis.

Investigating the association between an offender's sex and ethnicity and the sentence imposed at the Crown Court for drug offences

Sentencing Council for England and Wales · published 2020-01-15 · retrieved 2026-08-17

Commissioned Sentencing Council research (author: A. Isaac, 2020) using Crown Court Sentencing Survey (CCSS) data, April 2012 to March 2015, for supply, possession-with-intent-to-supply and conspiracy-to-supply offences of Class A/B drugs at the Crown Court — again an ETHNICITY analysis, not nationality. Two statistical models controlled for many (not all) factors judges must weigh: culpability, harm, aggravating/mitigating factors, guilty plea, and age. The Council states plainly that results "should not be regarded as conclusive" since not every factor judges consider could be included.

Odds of an immediate custodial sentence, by ethnicity, after controlling for the factors above: Asian and "Other" ethnicity offenders: 1.5x the odds of White offenders. Black offenders: 1.4x the odds of White offenders.

The Council's own worked example, quoted directly: for possession with intent to supply a Class B drug with the most common case factors, "around 37 White offenders, around 46 Asian offenders, around 46 Other ethnicity offenders and around 44 Black offenders" per 100 would be expected to receive immediate custody — an odds ratio of 1.5x does not mean "50% more people," a distinction the Council itself is careful to spell out.

What this data can and cannot tell you

  • Police recorded crime and the Crime Survey for England and Wales (CSEW) — the two main measures of crime in Britain, and the source of the falling-crime trend the original Independent article cited — do not record offender nationality at all. CSEW measures victims' experience of crime; police recorded crime measures reported/detected offences. Neither is an offender database.
  • The only routinely-published, nationality-broken-down official series is the MoJ prison population (source S1) — a snapshot of who is currently in custody, not a flow of convictions, and not the same population as "foreign national offenders removed from the UK" (S2) or "people convicted of a crime" (S3).
  • A raw nationality SHARE of the prison population is not the same as an offending RATE. The foreign-national population in the UK skews younger and more male than the general population, and young men of any nationality are more likely to be convicted of most offence types. The Migration Observatory's analysis (S5b) finds that once age and sex are controlled for, non-citizens are under-represented in the prison population, though this varies by offence type.
  • MoJ's main, routinely-published conviction and sentencing statistics (the "Outcomes by Offence" tool, source S4) do not include a nationality field at all — only offence, sex, age and ethnicity. This is a genuine, confirmed gap in the regular statistical series, not an oversight in this page's research.
  • The Ministry of Justice is appealing an Information Commissioner's Office order to disclose fuller conviction-by-nationality data (2018-2024) — the ICO ordered disclosure on 22 June 2026 (source S3), but as of 18 August 2026 the MoJ's appeal means that fuller dataset remains unpublished. The specific press figure of "14.1% of sexual offence convictions" could not be traced to an open, primary government document, and is not presented here as verified.
  • Prison and offender-return figures record legal NATIONALITY, not immigration status — a foreign national can hold indefinite leave to remain, refugee status, or British citizenship pending, and a British national is by definition excluded from every "foreign national" figure on this page regardless of their background. Nationality is not a proxy for recent migration.
  • 99.7% of the prison population has its nationality recorded (0.27% did not, as at 30 June 2026; around 0.4% in a June 2025 snapshot per the Migration Observatory) — every table on this page includes that small "not recorded" category rather than silently dropping it, which is a genuine data-quality gap, not a rounding artefact.
  • The "At a glance" tab compares a general-population estimate for calendar year 2024 (source S6, released 13 October 2025) against a prison-population snapshot as at 30 June 2026 — the best currently available matched pair, but around 20 months apart, not a same-day comparison. No more recent official nationality-by-population breakdown exists.
  • Sources S7, S8, S9 and S10 (the Ministry of Justice's Ethnicity and the Criminal Justice System 2024, the Lammy Review, the Home Office's stop-and-search/arrests statistics, and the Sentencing Council's drug-sentencing research) all measure ETHNICITY, not NATIONALITY. These are different, broader categories: British nationals of Black, Asian, Mixed or Other ethnicity are the overwhelming majority of "minority ethnic" people in this research, and none of it can be read as being specifically about foreign nationals. It is included because raw nationality-representation numbers alone cannot show whether the justice system treats people differently at the same stage for the same conduct — and the best official evidence on that question happens to be recorded by ethnicity, not nationality.
  • Stop-and-search and arrest statistics (source S9) carry no nationality breakdown at all, official or otherwise — checked directly against the Home Office's own release page. Ethnicity is the only demographic lens available at those two stages of the justice system.
  • The Sentencing Council's odds-ratio findings (source S10) apply specifically to drug supply-type offences at the Crown Court, using 2012-2015 data — not a general finding across all offence types, courts, or time periods. The Council's own paper states its results "should not be regarded as conclusive" because not every factor a judge considers when sentencing could be included in the statistical model.
